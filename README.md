# 🌾 Village Farm Advisory Kiosk

### Case Study 31 | AgriTech – Assisted Digital Service

**Go-to-Market & Customer Operations**

> **Making agricultural information accessible, trusted and affordable for every farmer.**

---

## 📌 Project Overview

The **Village Farm Advisory Kiosk** is an AgriTech assisted digital service designed to help farmers access important agricultural information without requiring them to use smartphone applications.

The kiosk provides farmers with:

- Weather forecasts
- Market and mandi prices
- Crop and pest advice
- Government scheme information

A trained local operator, called a **Krishi Sahayak**, helps farmers access and understand the information in their local language.

The service is designed especially for farmers who may have limited digital literacy, limited smartphone usage, language barriers or difficulty accessing reliable agricultural information independently.

---

## 🎯 Problem Statement

Many farmers still face difficulties accessing timely and reliable agricultural information.

Some common problems include:

- Limited digital literacy
- Difficulty using smartphone applications
- Language barriers
- Poor or unreliable internet connectivity
- Dependence on informal sources of information
- Travelling to mandis without knowing current prices
- Missing important weather information
- Difficulty accessing crop and pest guidance
- Missing government scheme and insurance deadlines

The Village Farm Advisory Kiosk addresses these problems by providing **human-assisted access to verified agricultural information at the village level**.

## 🔄 Customer Journey

The service follows a simple process:

**Awareness → Kiosk Visit → Registration → Request → Information Retrieval → Verification → Local-Language Explanation → ₹10 Payment → Printed Slip → Feedback → Record Update**

### How It Works

1. Farmers learn about the kiosk through village meetings, SHGs, panchayat announcements and awareness activities.
2. The farmer visits the kiosk.
3. The operator registers the basic details.
4. The farmer asks a question in their own language.
5. The operator identifies the information category.
6. Information is retrieved from approved sources.
7. The information is verified.
8. The operator explains the information in the local language.
9. The farmer pays ₹10 through cash or UPI.
10. A printed information slip containing the answer, source, date and time is provided.
11. Feedback is collected and the request is recorded.

## 🔐 Trust & Reliability Model

Trust is one of the most important parts of the proposed model.

The kiosk uses:

- A trained local Krishi Sahayak
- Panchayat / SHG endorsement
- Approved information sources
- Source and timestamp on every printed slip
- Weekly expert review of crop advice
- Fixed ₹10 pricing
- Payment receipts
- Complaint register
- ₹10 refund if an answer cannot be provided
- Consent-based farmer data handling
- No sale of farmer data

These measures are designed to make the kiosk a **trusted local information point for farmers**.

## 💰 Financial Model

The kiosk charges:

### **₹10 per information request**

### Financial Assumptions

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Price per request | ₹10 |
| Expected requests per day | 45 |
| Operating days | 30 days/month |
| Monthly operating cost | ₹9,000 |

### Revenue Calculation

**Daily Revenue**

45 requests × ₹10 = **₹450/day**

**Monthly Requests**

45 × 30 = **1,350 requests/month**

**Monthly Revenue**

1,350 × ₹10 = **₹13,500/month**

**Monthly Surplus**

₹13,500 − ₹9,000 = **₹4,500/month**

### Break-even

₹9,000 ÷ ₹10 = 900 requests/month

900 ÷ 30 = **30 requests/day**

Therefore, the kiosk needs approximately **30 requests per day to break even** under the 30-day operating assumption.

### Financial Summary

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Price per request | ₹10 |
| Expected requests/day | 45 |
| Daily revenue | ₹450 |
| Monthly requests | 1,350 |
| Monthly revenue | ₹13,500 |
| Monthly operating cost | ₹9,000 |
| Monthly surplus | ₹4,500 |
| Break-even | 30 requests/day |
| Margin of safety | 33% |
| Cost per request at 45/day | ₹6.67 |

> **Note:** If the kiosk operates for 26 days per month, the break-even requirement increases to approximately 35 requests per day.

## 📈 Capacity Analysis

At approximately **5 minutes per request**, an 8-hour working day can handle around **96 requests per day**.

The expected 45 requests per day represent approximately **47% utilisation**.

This indicates that **one trained operator can manage the expected pilot-stage demand**.

## 🔁 Customer Retention Strategy

### 1. Kisan Loyalty Card

Farmers can purchase a **₹100 prepaid card** that provides **11 requests**, making the 11th request effectively free.

Benefits:

- Encourages repeat visits
- Builds customer habits
- Rewards regular users
- Improves kiosk cash flow

### 2. Weekly Market-Day Alert & Crop Clinic

Registered farmers can receive weekly SMS or voice alerts containing:

- Market-price headlines
- Weather updates
- Crop information
- Weather warnings
- Government scheme reminders

A fixed crop clinic day can also provide personalised assistance.

## 📊 SWOT Analysis

### 💪 Strengths

- Affordable ₹10 pricing
- Trusted local operator
- Addresses a real information need
- Low fixed operating cost
- Works for farmers who cannot use smartphone applications

### ⚠️ Weaknesses

- Dependence on internet and electricity
- Seasonal variation in demand
- Dependence on operator skills
- Small initial revenue base
- Manual records may create errors

### 🚀 Opportunities

- Expansion to nearby villages
- Addition of e-services
- Partnerships with KVKs and FPOs
- Government digital-literacy initiatives
- Regional-language and voice-based services

### 🛡️ Threats

- Free mobile applications
- WhatsApp-based information
- Incorrect or outdated information
- Low farmer trust or footfall
- Weather and power disruptions
- Changes in data-access policies

---

## ⚠️ Risks & Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Wrong or outdated information | Approved sources, source/time on slip and expert review |
| Low customer footfall | Loyalty card, weekly alerts and SHG mobilisation |
| Internet failure | Mobile hotspot backup |
| Power failure | Power bank / inverter |
| Operator dependence | Training and backup operator |
| Cash leakage | UPI-first payments and daily cash log |
| Competition from free apps | Human assistance and local-language service |
| Data privacy concerns | Consent-based data handling and no data sale |
| Slow adoption by women and older farmers | SHG outreach and assisted demonstrations |

The recommended approach is to start with **one village**, validate the model and then expand.

- Identify the target village
- Understand farmer needs
- Study crops and farming seasons
- Understand existing information sources
- Partner with panchayat / SHG

### Phase 2 – Kiosk Setup

- Select and train the operator
- Set up equipment
- Arrange internet and printer
- Set up UPI payments
- Finalise approved information sources

### Phase 3 – Pilot Operation

- Conduct village demonstrations
- Start ₹10 paid requests

### Phase 4 – Feedback

Track:

- Information usefulness
- Information accuracy
- Waiting time
- Operator behaviour
- Most requested information categories
- Farmer satisfaction

### Phase 5 – Expansion

Based on pilot results:

- Improve the service
- Add weekly alerts

## 🏁 Conclusion

The **Village Farm Advisory Kiosk** provides a practical assisted-digital model for farmers who need agricultural information but may not be comfortable using smartphone applications.

The model combines:

- A trained local operator
- Approved information sources
- Local-language assistance
- Affordable ₹10 pricing
- Simple payment options
- Printed information slips
- Customer feedback
- Regular-use strategies

At **45 requests per day**, the proposed kiosk can generate **₹13,500 in monthly revenue against ₹9,000 in monthly operating costs**, resulting in a projected **₹4,500 monthly surplus**.

The break-even point is **30 requests per day** under the 30-day operating assumption.

The 90-day pilot focuses on proving three key points:

1. Farmers trust the information.
2. The kiosk can consistently reach the required request volume.
3. Farmers return and use the service regularly.

Once these conditions are demonstrated, the model can be expanded to nearby villages and additional services can be introduced.
